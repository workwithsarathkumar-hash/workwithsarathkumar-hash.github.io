What this actually is

The site stays. This is what you'd step into on purpose.

The idea isn't to replace the site you already know — the essays, the finance log, the parts of it that need to be read carefully still load in under two seconds on a phone at the default site. This is a separate mode you opt into: dark nebula and open space instead of a stack of sections.

What's rendering behind this text is a single continuous starfield, no cuts, no page breaks — scrolling down flies the camera forward through it, and each of the real sections (Hero, In the Wild, Responsible AI, Mindfulness, Words I Build By) is a distinct nebula cluster the camera arrives at along the way, each with its own color drawn from the same gold→rose→violet gradient. Scroll back up and the whole flight reverses — nothing is baked in one direction only.

The same scene is the pitch for AR, not a separate build: once the WebXR layer exists, this is what would render around you on Quest 3's passthrough — not a flat screen floating in your room, but the field itself, surrounding you, with your actual walls still visible through it.

  • liveWebGL mode — this scene, reachable from the main nav. Default site stays untouched.
  • nextWebXR enabler — immersive-AR session for Quest 3's passthrough, so the field renders into your actual room instead of a headset void.

Three.js / WebGL, no build step. This is a separate mode, not the default experience — the badge at the top takes you straight back.
