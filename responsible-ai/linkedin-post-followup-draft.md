I approved an action based on the AI's confident summary of it, not on checking the actual thing itself.

That's how my last LinkedIn post went out half-written.

Jarvis built it through a shell command with manually escaped quoting instead of writing the exact text to a file, then told me "matches exactly" based on a preview of the request — not the literal text that was about to post. The preview looked complete. What went out didn't. I caught it and deleted it myself.

Nothing serious happened here. A LinkedIn post is reversible in thirty seconds. But the shape of the mistake is exactly what the piece I'd just published was about: I trusted the AI's summary of an action instead of checking the actual thing. That gap is survivable when it's a LinkedIn post. It's the whole problem when it isn't reversible at all.

I added the full story as a postscript on the original piece, including my own honest take: I'm the one who writes about responsible AI, and in that moment, I wasn't responsible enough myself. I think that's a pattern in general — we lose patience, because the world around us moves fast, and we stop asking "am I doing this right, is it necessary right now, is it worth it right now." That pause is the part that actually matters.
